# Tranche C asset investigation, target asset schema and the closing-identity claim

Docs 41–43 and S11 investigate ERPNext's fourteen Assets parents at controller depth; doc 44 and `FINAL-SCHEMA.md` §19–§24 (the part added since `2d914f7`) turn that into a target schema with invariant register A1–A26. The upstream behavioural claims are accurate where I spot-checked them, all 4,437 citations resolve against the pinned trees, and the invariant register is internally consistent. The target schema, however, does not yet deliver the one thing the whole tranche is built around — the closing identity — and the worked numbers that motivate it are wrong in two places that change the story.

**Watch for:** the stranded-balance figures are wrong (**confirmed**) — S11 double-counts period 6, and the accumulated-depreciation residual is 3,000.00, not 3,858.37; it exactly offsets the fixed-asset residual, so net book value and gain/loss are *correct* upstream and only the account-level split is misstated. `gl_entry` has no asset dimension (**confirmed**), so every per-asset GL reconciliation check and the zero-balance acceptance criterion are uncomputable. The "one unreversed X" partial unique indexes are coded as "one non-reversing X" (**confirmed**), which makes reversal-then-redo structurally impossible for postings, disposals and recognitions. Split apportionment never reaches the depreciation ledger (**confirmed**), so a split target's disposal cannot zero the accumulated-depreciation account.

**Verdict**: NEEDS_CHANGES

## High-level view

The closing identity is the spine of Tranche C: doc 44 §1.1, `FINAL-SCHEMA` §19's preamble and §23's acceptance criterion all cite S11's `3,000.00` and `3,858.37` residuals as the failure the design removes. The second number is wrong. S11 §7 posts period 6 for the scrapped half only (858.36), but §8's proof charges 1,716.72 "both halves"; the sold half was `Sold` on 2026-05-31 and excluded from posting. Using S11's own figures the residual is 3,000.01, and algebraically it is always exactly `additional_asset_cost` — the same 3,000.00 stranded in Fixed Assets, with opposite sign. Net book value and the disposal gain/loss are therefore correct upstream; what is misstated is the split between cost and accumulated depreciation, because the capitalised repair is silently reclassified as depreciation. Still a real defect, but it needs restating in four documents.

The post-repair instalment in S11 §5.4 is also wrong: `97,946.23 / 57.0410959` is 1,717.12, not 1,716.72. The plan total survives because the residual row absorbs it; the instalment and final row do not.

On the target side, the per-asset GL reconciliation is the biggest gap. `gl_entry` carries `party_id`, `cost_center_id`, `project_id` and `finance_book_id` plus a generic `gl_entry_dimension`, but nothing identifies the asset. Every §20.4 continuous check, the §21.4 zero-balance deferred trigger and doc 44 §4.4's `fixed_asset balance(asset) = 0` need a per-asset GL balance that cannot be computed.

Three uniqueness constructs enforce something stricter than what they claim. `UNIQUE (…) WHERE reverses_posting_id IS NULL` and its disposal and recognition equivalents allow at most one *non-reversing* row, not one *unreversed* row, so once a posting, disposal or recognition is reversed the asset can never be re-posted, re-disposed or re-recognised — flows S11 §9 and doc 43 §6.2 treat as normal.

Split apportionment is stored and conserved between source and target, but nothing attributes the depreciation facts. A split target's `Σ depreciation_posting` is zero while GL holds its share of accumulated depreciation, so the fact-derived disposal legs reproduce exactly the residual the design was written to eliminate.

Multi-book behaviour is unspecified end to end. Docs 41–43 never state that upstream posts one depreciation journal per finance book, `asset_disposal` has no book dimension while `asset_transformation_part` does, and §21.3's `asset_service_book_apportionment` splits a cost addition across books when each book should absorb it in full.

Two fixed contracts are crossed. Asset events form a parallel stream with per-asset `event_no` and no `aggregate_owner_id`, `event_position`, `business_commit_id` or outbox row, yet §22 and doc 44 §5.2 promise outbox publication and `projection_checkpoint` advancement using §10's machinery, whose FKs point at `production_event`. And consumed-inventory value is frozen as a scalar under a hard equality constraint, contradicting §14's rule that every consumption value fact is a one-to-one allocation of an authoritative `stock_value_event`, so a backdated replay adjustment cannot be absorbed.

Status metadata has not caught up with commit `aa733e8`: `docs/logic/README.md`, `COVERAGE.md` and `INVESTIGATION-PLAN.md` all still describe Tranche C as docs 41–43 plus S11, and the plan's open question on localisation contradicts doc 44 §11.

Citation hygiene holds: `verify_refs.py` passes on 4,437 citations, the pinned hashes match the checked-out trees, A1–A26 are each declared exactly once, and all 26 names in doc 44 §3 match their source blockquotes character-for-character. One anchor is broken and three upstream claims are wrong.

## The stranded-balance figures, and what they actually prove

S11 §8 states the result the whole tranche is organised around:

```text
Fixed Asset — Machinery : 123,000.00 debited, 120,000.00 removed  → 3,000.00 stranded
Accumulated Depreciation:   8,487.21 posted,    4,628.84 removed  → 3,858.37 stranded
```

The 8,487.21 is `6,770.49 + 1,716.72`, where the second term is labelled "(period 6, both halves)". But ASS-0001 was sold on 2026-05-31 and `_update_asset` set its status to `Sold`; `get_depreciable_assets_data` selects only `Submitted` and `Partially Depreciated` assets (`assets/doctype/asset/depreciation.py:81-112`). Only ASS-0002 posts period 6, and §7 says so explicitly: 858.36. Total posted is 7,628.85, and the residual is `7,628.85 − 4,628.84 = 3,000.01`. The error is exactly 858.36 — the double-counted half.

The general result is stronger than the corrected number. Write `P` for total posted depreciation. Disposal debits accumulated depreciation with `net_purchase_amount − value_after_depreciation` per half, and the halves' book values sum to `123,000 − P`:

```text
accumulated debited = 120,000 − (vad₁ + vad₂) = 120,000 − (123,000 − P)
AccDep residual     = P − (120,000 − 123,000 + P) = 3,000.00
FA residual         = 123,000 − 120,000          = 3,000.00
```

The two residuals are always exactly `additional_asset_cost`, with opposite signs, independent of the instalment or the number of periods. Net book value removed is correct, the gain and loss are correct, and the 0.01 the arithmetic leaves behind is the rounding of the 50/50 split, not a defect in the subtraction.

That changes the finding, not its severity. What upstream actually does is post a capitalised addition to the fixed-asset account and then remove it from the accumulated-depreciation account at disposal — a silent reclassification of cost as depreciation, invisible in net book value and in the P&L, visible only in the two balance-sheet accounts and in every fixed-asset register and depreciation report built on them. Doc 44 §1.1's "Every individual voucher balances. The subsystem still fails to close" needs to become "every voucher balances and net book value closes; cost and accumulated depreciation are each misstated by the capitalised addition." `FINAL-SCHEMA` §19's preamble, §23's acceptance criterion, S11 §8, §10.4 and §12 item 8 all carry the same numbers.

## Post-repair instalment and the S11 numeric chain

S11 §5.4 derives the numbers correctly right up to the division. `get_number_of_pending_months` sets `total_months = 60 × 1 + 0` and `get_booked_depr_for_months_count` returns `(89 + 1) / (365/12) = 2.9589041`, so `pending_months = 57.0410959` — exactly as the doc says. `get_fixed_depr_amount` then divides:

```text
97,946.23 / 57.0410959 = 1,717.1169…  → 1,717.12      (doc says 1,716.72)
Σ rows 4..59 = 56 × 1,717.12 = 96,158.72              (doc says 96,136.32)
row 60 = 117,946.23 − 96,158.72 − 20,000.00 = 1,787.51 (doc says 1,809.91)
Σ all rows = 103,000.00  ✔
```

The `Σ = 103,000.00` identity holds either way because the salvage landing rule assigns the residual to the last row, which is worth keeping — it is the point of the section. Everything downstream shifts by cents: period 6 becomes 858.56, the halves become 58,114.56, and the residuals stay at 3,000.00.

S11 §10.4's "our design" figures are separately inconsistent. `4,243.61` is `2,526.89 + 1,716.72` — half of periods 1–4 plus the *full two-unit* period 5. Under one asset per unit (§10.1) period 5 for one unit is ~859, so accumulated removed is ~3,386 and the loss is ~13,114, matching §6.3's 13,114.76. As written the section implies a 12,256.39 loss on the same economics, and its intermediate NBV of 57,256.39 coincides suspiciously with the scrap NBV of §7.

§6.1's conservation claim fails in the direction the doc is trying to warn about. `116,229.51 / 2 = 58,114.755`; at two decimals both halves land on the same value, so they sum to 116,229.52 or 116,229.50. Doc 43 defect 24 ("two children need not sum to the parent") is demonstrated by this very example, and S11 §6.1 currently says the opposite.

## The salvage landing rule, described one step out of order

Doc 42 §4.1 gets the outcome right and the mechanism wrong:

```text
after 59 periods: accumulated = 59 × 1,666.67 = 98,333.53 ; pending = 21,666.47
period 60 raw    = 1,666.67
adjustment       = 21,666.47 − 20,000.00 − 1,666.67 = −0.20 relative to raw
final amount     = 1,666.67 + (21,666.47 − 20,000.00) − … → exactly 1,666.47
```

The third line reaches −0.20 by subtracting the raw amount separately; the fourth line, read as written, gives 3,333.14 and covers the gap with an ellipsis. The loop decrements pending *before* adjusting (`deppreciation_schedule_controller.py:87-92`, then `:364-379`):

```text
pending after decrement = 21,666.47 − 1,666.67 = 19,999.80
adjustment              = 19,999.80 − 20,000.00 = −0.20
final amount            = 1,666.67 − 0.20 = 1,666.47
```

The prose "it then adds `pending − salvage` to the current amount" is correct only for post-decrement pending, which the worked example contradicts by showing 21,666.47 as pending at that point. The section also omits `set_depreciation_amount_for_last_row`, which for a prorated schedule subtracts row 0's amount from the last row before the salvage adjustment (`:342-363`) — independently confirmed not to change the landing value, but it is the reason S11's plan has 61 rows rather than 60.

## The closing identity is not computable from `gl_entry`

`FINAL-SCHEMA` §20.4 lists six continuous deferred checks, three of which compare fact sums to GL:

> `Σ depreciation_posting.amount` (unreversed) **=** accumulated depreciation in `gl_entry` for that category's accumulated-depreciation account and finance book

and §21.4 closes with a deferred trigger asserting that "the fixed-asset and accumulated-depreciation balances for that asset must reach **zero** after the disposal commits". Doc 44 §4.4 and §10 step 10 make the same promise, and §23 names it as S11's acceptance criterion.

`gl_entry` (§3) carries `party_id, cost_center_id, project_id, finance_book_id`, a typed `settles_voucher_id`, `source_line_type`/`source_line_id`, and a generic `gl_entry_dimension(company_id, gl_entry_id, dimension_id, dimension_value_id)`. Nothing identifies the asset. "The fixed-asset balance for that asset" is not a query. Upstream at least has `against_voucher_type = 'Asset'` on the depreciation and repair legs (doc 42 §5.2, doc 43 §4.3), which the design replaces with the voucher-scoped `settles_voucher_id`.

Either add `asset_id bigint NULL REFERENCES asset(id)` to `gl_entry` with a check making it mandatory when `account_id` holds an asset role (fixed asset, construction in progress, accumulated depreciation, revaluation reserve, impairment, disposal gain/loss), or register `asset` as an accounting dimension and state that it is mandatory for those accounts plus the supporting index. Either way §20.4 and §21.4 need the mechanism named, because they are the only things standing between this design and the defect it was written to remove.

## Partial unique indexes that forbid correction

Three constructs share one bug:

```sql
depreciation_posting  UNIQUE (company_id, depreciation_period_id) WHERE reverses_posting_id IS NULL
asset_disposal        UNIQUE (company_id, asset_id)               WHERE reverses_disposal_id IS NULL
asset_recognition     UNIQUE (company_id, asset_id)               WHERE reverses_recognition_id IS NULL
```

Each admits at most one row that is not itself a reversal. Post a period, reverse it, and the corrected posting is refused — the index does not know the first row was reversed. Same for disposals: reverse a sale and the asset can never be disposed again, which is precisely the flow S11 §9 step 2 walks through ("a reversed sale leaves a split asset behind") and which doc 43 §6 documents as `restore_asset` and the regain legs. Same for recognition after a reversed acquisition.

The invariant wording is right — A12 says "at most one *unreversed* depreciation fact", A23 "exactly one unreversed disposal per asset". Postgres cannot express that as a partial unique index over an append-only table, because "unreversed" depends on the absence of a later row. The practical form is a deferred constraint trigger, evaluated while the asset (or asset/book) owner is locked, asserting that the count of rows not referenced by any `reverses_*` is at most one; the existing `disposal_no`/`recognition_no` sequences already give the ordering. Adding an explicit `state` column set by the reversal command works too, but reintroduces an update to a fact row, which §7 forbids. `maintenance_occurrence`'s `UNIQUE (company_id, maintenance_task_revision_id, due_date)` has the same shape: a cancelled occurrence blocks its due date permanently.

## Split apportionment stops short of the depreciation ledger

`asset_transformation_part` stores `apportioned_cost`, `apportioned_accumulated`, `apportioned_revaluation` and `apportioned_remaining_periods`, conserved per finance book by a deferred trigger with one `is_residual_part`. That is the right shape, and it correctly replaces upstream's float scaling.

Cost moves: `asset_cost_enum` has `transformation_in` and `transformation_out`, so the source's cost events net to zero and the targets acquire theirs. Accumulated depreciation has no equivalent. `depreciation_posting` rows belong to `depreciation_period` rows keyed on `(asset_id, finance_book_id, period_end)`, they are append-only, and there is no transformation posting kind. So after a split:

```text
target asset: Σ depreciation_posting  = 0
              apportioned_accumulated = its share of GL accumulated depreciation
disposal:     accumulated_removed = Σ depreciation_posting = 0
              → accumulated-depreciation balance for that asset ends non-zero
```

§20.4 check 1 fails for both source and target the moment a split happens, and §21.4's zero-balance trigger cannot pass. Upstream, oddly, does conserve this: `add_reference_in_jv_on_split` reduces the source's journal leg by exactly the amount it adds for the target (`assets/doctype/asset/mapper.py:362-390`), so total accumulated depreciation in GL is preserved and attributed per asset. Our design conserves the *apportionment record* but not the ledger attribution — a regression, not a wash.

The fix is a fifth term. Either emit an explicit `depreciation_attribution(asset_transformation_part_id, finance_book_id, amount)` fact and include it in check 1 and in `accumulated_removed`, or add signed transformation kinds to `depreciation_posting` (which then needs a matching relaxation of `CHECK (amount > 0)`). The same argument applies to `apportioned_revaluation` against `Σ asset_revaluation`.

Separately, the conservation trigger is closed over the transformation: it checks `Σ source = Σ target` per book but never that `Σ source` equals the source asset's actual measures at `effective_on`. A transformation that apportions the wrong total passes every constraint. Add the source-side equality against `Σ asset_cost_event`, `Σ depreciation_posting` and `Σ asset_revaluation` as of `effective_on`.

## Multi-book assets: the model is missing, and disposal is not book-scoped

Docs 41–43 record the multi-book *mechanism* — one `Asset Finance Book` row per book, one `Asset Depreciation Schedule` per book, `finance_book` on the depreciation journal — but never state the consequence: upstream posts a full depreciation journal for **every** finance book, so unfiltered GL accumulated depreciation is the sum across books while the fixed-asset cost is debited once. Any balance sheet that does not filter on finance book is wrong for a two-book asset. That is substantial upstream behaviour with no coverage in the tranche, and it is why the design's own checks disagree: §20.4 check 1 qualifies "and finance book", check 2 does not, and check 6 (`net_book_value = gross_cost − accumulated_depreciation + …`) is per book by its primary key while `gross_cost` is shared.

`asset_disposal` then carries a single `accumulated_removed`, `revaluation_released` and `gain_loss` with no `finance_book_id`, even though `asset_transformation_part` and `asset_revaluation` both have one and `depreciation_period` is keyed by book. For a two-book asset the disposal cannot state what each book removes, so the per-book reconciliation A14 and A25 promise has nothing to reconcile against. Add an `asset_disposal_book` child with a deferred check that its per-book sums match the four fact sums per book, and state which books post to GL.

The related conceptual problem is `asset_service_book_apportionment`:

```sql
asset_service_book_apportionment(…, finance_book_id, amount, asset_cost_event_id NOT NULL)
   -- deferred: Σ amount per allocation = allocation.amount  (exact)
```

Doc 43 §4.2 and defect 16 treat "the repair cost is added in full to every finance book's `value_after_depreciation`" as a defect, and this table splits 3,000 into 1,500 + 1,500 for two books. But cost is one number in one ledger, and each finance book is an independent measurement of the *same* asset under different rules; each book's carrying amount should rise by the full 3,000, exactly as `AssetRepair.update_asset_value` does. Splitting it makes both books understate cost and understate future depreciation. Conversely, if each book got a full 3,000 cost event, `cost_removed = Σ asset_cost_event` would double-count. The table conflates the single GL cost addition with each book's depreciable-base increase. Keep one book-agnostic `asset_cost_event` per addition and express the per-book effect as an input to the next `depreciation_plan` version (`opening_book_value` / `depreciable_base`), which §22's replanning step already produces. Doc 43 defect 16 should then be re-scoped to the life extension — where applying the same months to every book genuinely is a policy question — and to the GL double-count above.

## Where the design leaves the fixed accounting and stock contract

Two crossings, both with existing precedent in the document that the asset sections do not follow.

**Events and outbox.** §10 fixes one protocol: a fact transaction locks a `production_aggregate_owner`, takes `next_event_position`, inserts one `production_event` and exactly one `transactional_outbox` row referencing it, and a projector advances `projection_checkpoint(company_id, projector_code, aggregate_owner_id)` to the next contiguous position. `transactional_outbox.event_id` and `projection_checkpoint.aggregate_owner_id` are FKs into that structure. `asset_lifecycle_event` and `asset_custody_event` have `UNIQUE (company_id, asset_id, event_no)`, no `aggregate_owner_id`, no `event_position`, no `business_commit_id` and no outbox row — yet §22 says every asset command ends "→ outbox → commit → project", doc 44 §5.2 says asset facts "and their outbox events commit in one transaction", and `asset_book_value_projection.last_event_position` presumes a positioned stream. Either generalise `production_aggregate_owner`/`production_event`/`transactional_outbox`/`production_aggregate_enum` to domain-neutral names and make asset events rows in them, or state that assets have a second stream and restate the deferred triggers for it. `command_kind_enum` and `voucher_kind_enum` also need asset members (recognition, depreciation, revaluation, capitalisation, transformation, disposal), which no section mentions.

**Consumed inventory value.** §14 is explicit that a consumption value fact is a one-to-one allocation of an authoritative `stock_value_event`, that deltas are signed so backdating appends rather than mutates, and that the conversion equation sums unreversed deltas through the valuation watermark:

```sql
production_input_value(id, company_id, production_input_id, stock_value_event_id,
    realised_value_delta numeric(19,4), command_receipt_id, child_ordinal integer)
   UNIQUE (company_id, production_input_id, stock_value_event_id)
```

The asset equivalents are frozen scalars: `asset_capitalisation_stock(… stock_move_id, realised_value)` and `asset_service_cost_allocation(source_kind='stock_issue_move', … amount)`, under a hard deferred equality `total_value = Σ realised_value + … = Σ asset_cost_event.amount`. A backdated cost change on the consumed lot appends a `backdate_adjustment` `stock_value_event` and a linked adjustment voucher; the capitalised asset cost cannot follow, so §20.4 check 2 (`Σ asset_cost_event = gl_entry movement on the fixed-asset account`) breaks and the equality constraint becomes unsatisfiable. `asset_cost_enum` has no adjustment member either. Mirror `production_input_value`: an `asset_capitalisation_stock_value` (and service equivalent) keyed by `stock_value_event_id` with a signed delta, a `backdate_adjustment` cost kind, and the total constraint restated over unreversed deltas through the watermark.

Tenant context is the one contract area with nothing to raise: §19's preamble and doc 44 §2 both restate `company_id` everywhere, company-scoped keys, `ENABLE`/`FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` and policies bound to authenticated tenant context rather than a caller-settable GUC.

## Constraints that are weaker than the invariant they are cited for

A1's "the number of assets recognised from a purchase line can never exceed the quantity that line actually received" is enforced by `asset_source_allocation`'s residual trigger. But doc 41 §9 defect 1 — the defect that motivates the whole table — is "the item code is not compared", and no constraint requires the source line's item to equal `asset.item_id`. `qty` is nullable with no conditional NOT NULL for `source_kind = 'purchase_line'`, so a purchase-line allocation can carry an amount with no quantity and slip past the quantity half of the residual check.

A19's custody chain is checked by an L2 trigger described as requiring `from_*` to "equal the current projection". Doc 44 §7 states that a projection "can lag or be rebuilt; it cannot authorise, recognise, depreciate, value or post." Continuity must be validated against the latest unreversed `asset_custody_event` under the asset lock, not against a projector-written row. The declarative checks are also weaker than doc 43 §8.1 claims: `CHECK (num_nonnulls(to_location_id, to_custodian_id, from_location_id, from_custodian_id) > 0)` requires a dimension to be *present*, not to *change*, and only the location inequality is present — the custodian inequality doc 43 promises is missing.

A11's exactness rests on §20.2's deferred constraints, the second of which cannot be evaluated:

```text
max(planned_accumulated) + salvage_value = opening_book_value + Σ future cost events
```

A deferred constraint is checked at commit; "future cost events" do not exist then, and the moment one is appended the constraint fails for an already-committed plan. State it as `= opening_book_value` at generation and rely on §22's rule that a cost change generates plan version N+1.

Nothing closes a disposed asset. §20.4 check 5 forbids postings beyond the plan's `final_period_end`, but the plan is marked `closed`, not deleted, and no rule forbids inserting a backdated `asset_cost_event`, `depreciation_posting` or `asset_revaluation` dated before an unreversed disposal. Any such insert permanently breaks the zero-balance assertion §21.4 makes a deferred trigger. Add a rule refusing facts dated at or before an unreversed disposal's `effective_on`, so a correction must reverse the disposal first — which §6's reversal order already implies.

Composite assets have a smaller version of the same gap. §19.3 requires consuming an asset to insert a real `asset_disposal`, and `asset_disposal.voucher_id` is `NOT NULL`; a `Composite Component` was never separately recognised (doc 41 §4.3 records that upstream deliberately excludes it from the target debit), so its cost, accumulated depreciation and gain/loss are all zero and its fixed-asset balance was always zero. Either allow a null voucher for a zero-value disposal or add an explicit component-absorption disposal kind with its own derivation. Nothing constrains `asset_component.removed_at` against the parent's disposal either, so a parent can be disposed with members still attached.

## Upstream claims: three corrections and one missing defect

`verify_refs.py` passes cleanly (4,437 citations, 0 problems, 0 name notes) and the pinned hashes match the checked-out trees, so this is about semantics, not resolvability. The high-risk claims I traced end to end are accurate: `validate_make_gl_entry`'s four `exists` probes and the invoice branch falling through to an implicit `None` (`asset.py:859-898`); `make_post_gl_entry`'s `= nowdate()` filter inside a CWIP-only category loop (`asset.py:1085-1103`); `get_asset_details` deriving accumulated depreciation by subtraction (`depreciation.py:696-716`); `get_companies_with_frozen_limits` calling `frappe.get_roles()` in the running session (`depreciation.py:113-127`); `calculate_next_due_date`'s freshly-computed value inside the terminating `or` (`asset_maintenance.py:97-133`); the unbound `credit_entry`/`debit_entry` at zero difference (`asset_value_adjustment.py:86-130`); the movement recompute's `company` scope, `transaction_date`-then-`name` ordering and `if location` guard while custodian is written unconditionally (`asset_movement.py:128-160`); `_get_total_days`'s `(row_idx − 1) * frequency` (`controller.py:429-436`); the shift-factor map inversion and exact-match `throw` (`asset_shift_allocation.py:76-130`); and the full-amount writes to every finance book in `update_asset_value` and `set_increase_in_asset_life`.

Three claims need changing.

**Doc 41 §5.2, final paragraph.** "Both of those run from `on_update` … so an ordinary save of a *submitted* asset can rewrite finance-book counters and create or regenerate schedule documents." Frappe runs `on_update` only for `_action in ('save', 'submit')`; saving a docstatus-1 document sets `_action = 'update_after_submit'` and runs only `on_update_after_submit` (`frappe/model/document.py:1447-1450`, `frappe/model/document.py:1889-1897`), which `Asset` does not define. So `create_asset_depreciation_schedule`, `validate_expected_value_after_useful_life` and `set_total_booked_depreciations` do **not** fire when `AssetRepair.update_asset_value` or `set_split_asset_values` save a submitted asset — the replanning that does happen is the explicit `reschedule_depreciation` call. The correct statement is "on any draft save and again at submit", and the submitted-asset case is a different and sharper problem: `flags.ignore_validate_update_after_submit` writes amount fields with no validation and no derivation at all.

**Doc 43 §5 and S11 §6.1 understate `add_reference_in_jv_on_split`.** It is described as amending the journal "to reference the new asset for its share of the amount". It also flips `docstatus` to 2, calls `make_gl_entries(1)` to cancel the posted GL rows, flips back to 1 and re-posts them (`assets/doctype/asset/mapper.py:345-360`). Posted GL is cancelled and rewritten in place — a much sharper violation of the append-only contract than "amended". Worth adding for the opposite reason too: `adjust_account_balance` reduces the source leg by exactly the amount `add_new_entries` adds for the target (`mapper.py:362-390`), so total accumulated depreciation in GL *is* conserved. Without that sentence a reader reasonably assumes the split loses depreciation, and it is the fact that makes the split-apportionment gap above a regression rather than a wash.

**Doc 41 §2.4 omits a filter.** `update_maintenance_status` scans only assets with `maintenance_required = 1` and no `disposal_date` (`asset.py:1070-1082`). Both doc 41 §2.4 and doc 42 §5.1 use this scan to explain how a mutable status suppresses depreciation posting; the story needs `maintenance_required` set. The unconditional route to `Out of Order` is `AssetRepair.update_status` from `validate` (`asset_repair.py:177-187`), which doc 43 §4.1 and S11 §5.1 cite correctly.

Two smaller slips: doc 41 §3.2's table row "Composite Asset | none | always post" is contradicted by `make_gl_entries`, which first calls `check_asset_capitalization_gl_entries` and returns early both when the capitalisation already debited the target account and when no submitted capitalisation exists (`asset.py:984-1001`) — §3.3 partly corrects it, so the table row should not say "none". And doc 41 §5.2 omits the `if self.split_from: return` guard at the head of `set_depr_rate_and_value_after_depreciation` (`asset.py:219-221`), which is what stops a split child's scaled book value being overwritten by `net_purchase_amount − opening + additional`.

One defect appears to be missing from doc 41 §9. `set_asset_values` and `calculate_totals` fix `item.asset_value`, `total_value` and `target_incoming_rate` during validate (`asset_capitalization.py:310-320`, `:339-364`). At submit, the GL composer calls `depreciate_asset(asset, doc.posting_date)` and reloads *before* building the disposal legs, then passes the stale `item.asset_value` as the selling amount (`services/gl_composer.py:82-116`), and `get_gl_entries_on_asset_disposal` computes `profit_amount = selling_amount − value_after_depreciation` against the freshly reduced book value (`depreciation.py:639-695`). If any period fell due between save and submit, the composite build books a phantom gain on the consumed asset and debits the target with the pre-depreciation value. Doc 41 §4.2 and §4.3 describe both halves without connecting them. Confidence: **likely** — I traced both call sites and the value flow, but did not execute it.

## Doc 43's split example mislabels accumulated depreciation

Doc 43 §5's worked split of "10 units at 120,000.00 total, 40,000.02 accumulated, into 3 and 7" says "opening/accumulated scaled". `set_split_asset_values` scales `opening_accumulated_depreciation` — zero for an ordinary purchased asset — and `value_after_depreciation`; there is no accumulated-depreciation field on `Asset` (`assets/doctype/asset/mapper.py:232-250`). For an asset whose 40,000.02 came from posted depreciation rather than an opening balance, nothing labelled "accumulated" is scaled at all; the effect arrives indirectly through `value_after_depreciation` and directly through the journal amendment. The example reads as though accumulated depreciation were a stored, scaled quantity, which is exactly the confusion the surrounding prose warns against.
